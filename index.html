Top tips before you rack 'em up

New to English pool? These three things will save you a surprising amount of swearing.

💥

Learn the break

A legal break needs three points. A potted ball counts as one point and each object ball that crosses an imaginary centre line counts as one. Smashing them hard isn't enough on its own.

🎯

Use the aiming help

If you're learning, use the game's aiming assistance by clicking Game Menu during a match to understand where your shot is heading. It's there to help — and you can reduce or disable assistance as you improve.

🤏

Don't just use 100% power

Maximum power is rarely the best choice. Softer, controlled shots make pots easier to manage and leave the cue ball somewhere useful for your next shot. Save the big hits for when you actually need them.

Gameplay FAQs

The questions most likely to occur immediately after accusing the game of cheating.

How do I make a legal break?

You need to score at least three break points. Each object ball potted scores one point, and each object ball that crosses the imaginary centre line of the table scores one point. If the total is fewer than three, the break is illegal and the game will explain what happens next.

What sort of power should I use?

Think control first. Most ordinary pots don't need anything close to maximum power. Use enough to make the shot comfortably while thinking about where the cue ball will finish. The break is an obvious time for a stronger shot, but even then power isn't a substitute for a legal break. Find your sweet spots - 70% to 90% is typical for a nice break, and shots after that work well at 30% to 50% depending on the situation. Even a 15% shot helps in tight situations if you want to cuddle the ball against or into the pocket instead of ricochet it off into the unknown.

How can I get help aiming?

Use the game's aiming assistance and angle guide whilst you're learning. They help show the direction of the shot and make it easier to understand contact angles. The assistance can be changed or switched off when you want more of a challenge - or if you're playing local head to head with a friend and REALLY want to show off.

Why didn't I get two shots after a foul?

Not every turn-ending event is treated as the same kind of foul, so an opponent losing their turn does not automatically mean you receive two visits. Check the on-screen foul/message text and game log: when a foul awards two visits, the game will show that extra visit as part of the turn.

What's a 'visit'?

A visit is your turn at the table. During a visit, you keep playing shots for as long as you legally pot your balls. Your visit ends when you fail to pot, commit a foul, or finish the game.

Why didn't I get that colour?

After the break the table can remain open. Simply potting a red or yellow doesn't always immediately assign that group. The game will tell you when groups have been decided, so until then, you're still choosing from an open table.

What happens after a foul?

The game handles the appropriate foul consequence automatically and tells you what has been awarded. Depending on the situation this can include an additional visit and, where the rules allow it, cue-ball placement. Watch the status message before taking the next shot.

Can I turn the aiming guide off?

Yes. The aiming help is there for players who want it, not as a requirement. You can change the aim-guide setting as you become more confident. Captain Blackball doesn't care if you have it on or off, and it doesn't affect progression or trophies if you have it on.

Does my progress save?

Yes. Progress is stored locally on your device. The game also supports save export and import, so it's worth keeping an exported backup before uninstalling the game or making major changes to your device.

Can I play offline?

Normal gameplay is designed not to need a continuous internet connection once the game is installed. External web links and services naturally still need internet access.

What's different about English rules pool?

If you're used to American-style 8-ball, some familiar situations work differently — particularly the break, fouls, and visits. Seamen Pool handles the rules for you, so the status messages are a useful guide while you're learning. For bedtime reading, we've included a link to the full official English Pool Association rules in the game's main menu. They're only actually around two pages long at the moment.
